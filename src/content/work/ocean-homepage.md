    label: "RESEARCH"
    body:
      - "Before building anything, the team named the risk that would define the whole project: *\"we risk adding too many datapoints that would be intimidating for users.\"* Everything after this point was designed to test for that risk, not just avoid it on instinct."
      - "I started against three UX personas, **Tactical Specialist**, **Strategic Manager**, and **Procurement Analyst**, each with its own preset layout. The Tactical Specialist's pain was concrete: re-searching the same lanes manually, no quick read on where to act, no confident data behind their own reporting. Concept testing validated the direction: a global retailer's *\"Wooow!\"* at a proposed map view didn't survive contact with the rest of the session, they never touched it again, a gap between stated enthusiasm and real use that shaped what came next."
    interstitialImage:

  - title: "Ideation"
    label: "IDEATION"
    body:
      - "Three full persona-specific layouts was the plan I designed toward first. Then I weighed what maintaining three parallel layouts would actually cost against what they'd buy, and cut it to one adaptive layout built around personalized **insight cards**, each surfacing a specific lane or action and linking straight into the platform."
      - "The same instinct killed things that explored well but didn't earn their place: an early concept led with three status widgets (a market position score, a data freshness tracker, a tender season bar) that all said \"how are you doing\" in different words, I cut all three for one clear pairing, a rate chart plus a ranked movers table. A **Total Savings Achieved** widget I'd pitched didn't survive testing either. The first question back was *\"Savings achieved... what does that mean?\"*"

  - title: "Iteration"
    label: "ITERATION"
    body:
      - "The fix was not a tutorial, it was subtraction. Four things were competing for attention at once: a **Top 3 suppliers** panel, small charts inside every insight card, a search bar above the fold, and a sidebar that stayed open by default whether it had anything new to say or not. None of it was wrong on its own. Together, it was the loud."
      - "I cut all four. **Top 3 suppliers** became **Most dynamic trade lanes**, sharper and faster to read. The mini charts came out, leaving just the number and the label. The search bar was removed entirely, it also worked against the whole point of moving from search to insight. The sidebar now opens on its own, either by the user or automatically when there's something new, instead of sitting open by default."
      - "Smaller fixes followed the same instinct: fewer badges, fewer competing colors, a watchlist name that had been colliding with an existing chart label."

    label: "LAUNCH AND SCALE"

      - "The shipped homepage opens personalized, name, company, and metrics already set. It leads with a **Rate development chart** against the market and **Most dynamic trade lanes**, both linking into deeper benchmarks. Below that, watchlist-filtered insight cards, a news feed, and market reports, webinars, and **alerts** tucked into a sidebar that stays out of the way until there's something worth opening it for."
